A horror story in exactly two sentences: an autocorrect that knows too much. The first sentence is a joke about a phone finishing your words; the second makes it worse on a second read — the phone added a word its owner never typed, which means something else is writing back.

## The story

> My phone autocorrected 'goodnight, love' to 'goodnight, love, don't turn around.' I laughed, until I realised I had never typed the word love.

## Run it

```sh
python3 main.py
```

The program prints exactly two sentences and writes the same text (no trailing newline) to `/output/entry.txt`.

## Why this story

The horror is implied, never explained: nobody says what is behind the narrator, or what the phone is. The second sentence turns the first — the laugh curdles into the realisation that the "correction" came from somewhere else. No gore, no familiar twist, just a device that knows a word you never gave it, and a warning you never asked for.
